The private window

A window, not a toggle

⇧⌘N. Everything in it is private — its tabs and its spaces —, without history, never written to the session. A private/normal toggle would lie both ways.

An ephemeral data store

Its tabs share a data store that disappears with the window: you sign in in one, the link opened next to it is signed in too, then nothing remains.

Nothing through the side doors

A closed private tab doesn't come back with ⇧⌘T, a site's zoom stays in memory, a permission only counts for this once, favicons don't go to disk, a saved file doesn't keep the address it came from, and the window isn't handed to macOS's window restoration.

The page's window decides

A private page that finishes loading while you're looking at an ordinary window doesn't enter the history. A link coming from another app goes to a normal window, and a window opened by a private page stays private.

Cookies

Rejecting banners

Wuji clicks “Reject all” — or calls the interface meant for it — on the consent tools it knows: Didomi, OneTrust, Cookiebot, Usercentrics, Sirdata, CookieYes, Complianz, Google, YouTube, Bing, Amazon… Rejecting, not hiding: a hidden banner leaves the decision pending.

Never at random

Only a banner waiting for an answer, never a choice already made, and no general heuristic: a click in the wrong place would accept on your behalf. “Accept or subscribe” walls stay visible — they offer no way to refuse.

Blocking third-party cookies

A site embedded in another's page — an ad, a “Like” button, a player — neither sets nor reads a cookie. Turned off, an embedded payment that needs one works.

Site by site

In the padlock menu: block third-party cookies or all cookies for this site alone, and erase what it left behind.

Tracking

Preventing cross-site tracking

WebKit's tracking prevention, the one in Safari.

Slowing tracking scripts

WebKit's protection against scripts recognized as trackers.

Private click measurement

On, as in Safari; off, no ad attribution report leaves.

Do not sell or share my data

The full Global Privacy Control signal, set by WebKit: the Sec-GPC: 1 header on every request — the one California law and websites read — and navigator.globalPrivacyControl in every frame.

Links and redirects

Removing trackers from links

WebKit only removes three. Wuji also removes those from HubSpot, Mailchimp, Marketo, Instagram, TikTok, Yandex, Twitter, LinkedIn and the utm_*, before the page loads. Neither ref nor Amazon's tag: those are intended referrals.

Skipping tracking redirects

A link from Google, Facebook, YouTube, Reddit, Steam, DuckDuckGo, LinkedIn, Slack or VK no longer goes through them first: the destination is in the address, so you go straight there.

Holding back unrequested redirects

A click on an empty spot of a page — or on a transparent layer laid over it — no longer takes the tab to an advertiser. A real link or button always goes through.

HTTPS first

An http:// address is tried first as https://, and falls back on request if the site doesn't allow it.

Data and history

Site by site

Privacy lists each domain with what it left behind — cookies, storage, cache —, a field to find it, one button per row. Erasing a row signs you out of that site, and that site only.

On quit

Site data can be erased each time you quit; Wuji waits for WebKit to finish before quitting.

The history you keep

Its duration can be set; beyond it, pages are erased at the next launch. Erasing it also erases what told its story: the favicons of visited sites, the sites you typed, and WebKit's records that list domains — HSTS, tracking prevention.

Your files, readable

Favorites, session, reading list: JSON files in ~/Library/Application Support/Wuji that you can read and edit by hand. Only the vault is encrypted.

What never happens

No telemetry

The only request Wuji makes on its own behalf is the version check — without an identifier, without saying which one you use, and only on a click, unless you allow it at launch.

No search engine suggestions

They mean sending every keystroke to a third party, including what you erase before pressing Return. The palette searches only on your Mac.

Favicons come from the site

Never from a third-party service that would learn along the way what you visit.

Only Wuji's pages talk to Wuji

What sets, erases or installs only answers to the main frame of a wuji:// page. An ordinary page can't turn anything off with a message.

Pre-warming, knowingly

Pre-warming connections saves about 150 ms, but the site knows you're coming before your click. It only targets sites you've already visited, never from a private window, and can be turned off in Performance.
