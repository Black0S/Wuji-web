On first launch

Wuji starts empty, and that's on purpose: nothing is chosen for you. A few gestures make it yours.

Choosing your blocking

Settings › Blocking: “Recommended selection” installs nine lists in one click, or check your own in uBlock Origin's catalog.

Bringing your favorites

“Import…” on the favorites page reads those from Chrome, Brave, Edge, Arc, Vivaldi and Safari, or an HTML export.

Creating your vault

Settings › Passwords: a master password, Touch ID if you want, and importing the CSV export from the Passwords app.

Making it the default browser

Settings › General, in one click: links from other apps open in Wuji.

Updates

Checking, whenever you want

Settings › General asks for the latest published version — without an identifier, without saying which one you use: the comparison happens on your Mac.

Nothing installs itself

A new version opens its page, and you decide. Checking at launch is off by default.

Your data

On your Mac, readable

Everything lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Wuji: favorites, session, reading list in JSON you can read by hand, history in SQLite, and the vault, encrypted.

Removing everything

Drag Wuji to the Trash, then that folder: nothing else remains except what WebKit stores for sites, which can also be erased in Settings › Privacy before you go.

License

MIT

Wuji can be read, modified, redistributed and reused freely, on the sole condition of keeping the copyright notice. Only the code copied from uBlock Origin stays under GPL-3.0, with its license.

No dependencies

The public suffix list comes from swift-psl, and the countermeasure code from uBlock Origin; both live in the sources, copied with their license. SwiftPM puts a dependency's resources where macOS refuses to sign: the choice was between the dependency and distribution.
