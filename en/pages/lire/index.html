Reading and keeping

Read a page the way you want, keep it for later, find it again, take it with you. Nothing you read goes off to someone to be cleaned up.

Dark pages, under the moon

The moon in the top bar makes the page dark — without reloading, and without inverting the photos.

The site's version, or a built one

“Dark” takes the site's dark version if it has one — GitHub, Python's or Apple's documentation are recognized and left as they are —, and builds one otherwise. “Turn off on this site” keeps the original where it looks better.

Without reloading

The page changes before your eyes: a half-filled form stays that way.

Element by element, not a filter

Every background, text, border and gradient is reread as the page computes it, then rewritten darker while keeping its hue. An orange button stays orange, a photo is never touched, a dark icon is inverted to stay visible.

It follows the page

A menu that opens, a style sheet that arrives late, a style a framework rewrites, the ::before: everything is picked up as it goes, in 12 ms batches, without the page flashing white first.

Measured, text by text

A bench of fifteen trap pages measures the contrast of each text against its actual background: 0 unreadable texts out of 5,200, no page left light.

Reader mode

The article, and nothing else

⇧⌘R. The block is picked by its link density: a menu, a footer, a column of recommendations are made of links, a paragraph has almost none.

Nothing leaves

Read-later services send the address, sometimes the whole page, to someone to clean it up. Here, the machine that displays is the one that cleans.

The reading list

Offline

⇧⌘L sets the page aside, with its article: you reread it without a network from wuji://reading, where what's left to read comes before what's been read.

Read later

On a link, “Read Later” keeps the address without opening the page. The list also opens in the right column.

A saved article can't run anything

Scripts, frames, on… attributes and javascript: links are removed, and the article's page forbids any script through its security policy.

Favorites and history

Sorted favorites

⌘D sets the page aside, ⇧⌘B opens the favorites. Folders and subfolders, sorting by dragging or with “Move to…”.

The page, not the section

A favorite keeps the page: a section anchor (#raccourcis) is removed, the one of an app that routes by anchor (#/inbox) is kept.

Importing from another browser

Chrome, Brave, Edge, Arc and Vivaldi are read in place; Safari too when macOS allows it; any browser's HTML export can be picked by hand. No duplicates, and the count is announced before writing.

The whole history

⌘Y opens wuji://history: the whole history, day by day, with a search. It's kept as long as you choose, and is erased line by line or all at once.

Downloads

What the web asks for

A link with the download attribute, a file served as an attachment, a blob: a script clicks: all of them download, under the name the page asked for.

Resuming after a cut

An interrupted download survives quitting, marked “interrupted”, with its button. Nothing resumes on its own: resuming two gigabytes over a tethered connection would be a decision you didn't make.

Names that don't step on each other

Two files with the same name started in the same second no longer overwrite each other, and a name that would try to leave the folder is cut down to its last segment.

In the column

⌘J opens the downloads page; the right column shows them too, and the button at the foot of the column shows their progress.

Save, print, capture

Saving the page

⌘S: a web archive to keep it browsable, a PDF to freeze it — the whole page, not just the visible area.

Printing

⌘P, with the system's print panel.

The whole page

Scrolled through screen by screen then stitched back together, as you see it: the playing video and the images that only load as you approach are there.

An area, an element

You draw an area, or point at an element by hovering — ↑ and ↓ widen or narrow it. An element taller than its window, like a chat thread, is scrolled through where it scrolls.

Saved and copied

Each screenshot goes to the downloads and to the clipboard. No shortcut: ⇧⌘3, ⇧⌘4 and ⇧⌘5 belong to the system.
