Windows, spaces and folders

Tabs belong to a space, each window has its own, and each space can be organized into folders.

Windows, each with its spaces

⌘N opens one, ⇧⌘W closes it. Closing a window closes its tabs — and File › Reopen Closed Window brings it back with its spaces. At launch, they all come back to their place.

Spaces

⌥T creates one, ⌥W closes it, ⌥]/$ and ⌥[/^ move from one to the other — the same keys as for tabs, the other modifier. Switching spaces shows: the column slides in from the side it comes from.

A name, a symbol

The column's title opens the map of spaces: their name, their symbol, the number of tabs in each. You reorder them by dragging and rename them in place.

Space templates

A recipe rather than a space: opening it creates a new one, with its tabs. You compose one, or save the open space (Settings › Spaces).

Tab folders

⌥⌘N creates a folder; a tab goes in by dragging, and the folder collapses. Right-clicking on an empty spot of the column offers new tab, reopen closed tab, new folder.

Nothing closes by surprise

Closing a space asks nothing, but nothing is lost: the history keeps its pages, and the previous session is kept next to the current one.

Two columns, three widths

Tabs on the left, what you've set aside on the right. Each one can be adjusted, moved, or hidden.

Wide, collapsed, on hover

Each column has three widths: full, collapsed to its icons, or collapsed and opening when the pointer comes near. They're chosen under the column's title, in one click.

The right column

Favorites, history, downloads or the reading list — one at a time, chosen under its title. Each one can be searched, and the history can be browsed in full, not just its latest pages.

Sorting by dragging

Favorites and their folders are sorted like tabs: a gap opens where they'll land, the middle of a folder drops them inside.

Pulling onto the page

A page from the right column dragged onto the left or right half of the page opens next to the one you're reading; if there's no page, it opens in its place.

Swapping the two columns

Tabs on the right and lists on the left, if that's how you work: one setting, and everything follows — the gaps, the openings on hover.

Customize the interface

A card set in the middle of the window: you drag the top bar's buttons onto it, choose the widths and the right-hand list, while watching the interface change behind.

Interface zoom

From 80 to 200%, for the column, the bar and the panels, without touching the pages: the chrome is redrawn to scale, not enlarged like an image.

Scrolling like the page

Columns scroll with the system's physics — the wheel's damping, the trackpad's momentum, the bounce at both ends. A column where everything fits doesn't move.

The address bar and the palette

One field for everything: an address, a search, a tab already open, a page from yesterday.

Three gestures, not one more

The palette opens with ⌘T, ⌘L and a click on the address. Never on its own: you had just closed something, not asked for a field.

Completing as you type

“you” becomes “youtube.com”, the end selected: Return goes there, one more key replaces it, ⌫ erases it. Only toward a whole site you've already typed yourself: “paypal.com” never becomes “paypal.com.pirate.net”, nor “paypal” a lookalike opened from an email.

Suggestions that stay on your Mac

Open tabs, favorites, and the history if you turn it on — over the period and in the number you choose. No keystroke goes off to a search engine.

Five engines, and your own

Google, DuckDuckGo, Qwant, Bing and Luxxle, each with its keyword: @d mozart searches DuckDuckGo whatever the default engine. You can add some, remove some.

A field that doesn't move

It takes the whole length between the buttons, and its place only depends on the window. The padlock is on the left and stays there: a security indicator that moves has to be found before it can be read.

The zoom badge

When a site differs from the default zoom, a badge says so in the bar, and one click restores it. No difference, no badge.

Two pages side by side

Read one while writing in the other, compare two versions, follow a video next to some documentation.

Split, undo

⇧⌘D splits the view with a new tab, and closes it again. Undoing a pair closes nothing: both tabs stay where they were.

Dragging a tab or a favorite

Dragged over the page, it brings up two cards, “Left” and “Right”; dropped on one, it opens on that side.

The proportion you want

The divider can be dragged; a double-click puts it back in the middle. The session brings back the pair and its proportion at the next launch.

The bar follows the active pane

You choose a pane by clicking in it: the address, find in page, zoom, reader mode and ⌘W target it, without knowing anything about the pair.

Zen mode

The page, alone, to the edges of the window — or of the screen.

Everything fades away

The columns and the bar leave; the page takes the whole window, corners included. In full screen, it touches the edges of the screen.

The title bar comes back when you look for it

The pointer on the traffic-light area, or against the top edge: the bar appears, can be dragged to move the window, and fades half a second later.

The exit and the zoom, joined in the corner

A single tab at the bottom right carries the Zen mode exit and the zoom slider, with the same rounding as the rest of the interface.

Zoom

Two zooms that don't mix: the pages', remembered per site, and the interface's.

Per-site zoom

⌘+ and ⌘− adjust the site you're looking at, and it remembers; ⌘0 gives it back the default zoom. Only differences are kept — a site that's hard to read doesn't impose its fix on the whole web.

A slider on the page

A zoom slider can sit at the bottom right of the page, attached to its edge: it sets the same per-site zoom, in the same steps.

The difference is measured against your setting

The badge and the slider compare against the default zoom you chose, not against one hundred percent.

Tabs, sound and sessions

Reopening what you closed

⇧⌘T reopens the last closed tab. The session is saved on every change, and the previous one is kept alongside: a failed write becomes a rename, not a loss.

A tab that plays glows

Its outline lights up in the column. A speaker appears in the address field: mute, pause, play, picture in picture — even when the player lives in a frame from another site.

Sound is cut in the engine

The video keeps going, the page sees nothing and can't turn the sound back on by itself.

One Wuji page per space

Favorites, history, settings: shortcuts join the tab already open on a Wuji page rather than stacking up another one.

Mac gestures

Swiping with two fingers goes back — and can be turned off if your wide tables start navigating. The scroll bar can be hidden.

The system's menus

The menus are macOS's — keyboard, VoiceOver, submenus included —, with their icons, which macOS 27 usually hides. Wuji's own questions arrive in a bubble at the bottom right, and closing without answering means “no”.

French or English

Settings › General › Language: “System” follows the Mac, otherwise French or English, at the next launch. Units, decimal separators and dates follow Wuji's language. One more language means one more file to translate.
