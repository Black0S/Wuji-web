Sites and security

Per-site settings

For all, then for each

JavaScript, pop-ups, autoplay, for all sites; then the list of sites that have their own, each one erasable. “Settings for this site”, in the padlock menu, sets the one you're looking at.

Autoplay “muted only”

By default, like Safari: a video with sound waits, a muted one plays; “Never” stops both.

JavaScript turned off for a site

The page's scripts no longer run. Wuji's own pages keep theirs, whatever you set.

Present as

Safari, Chrome or Firefox, for sites that refuse what they don't recognize. The engine stays WebKit.

Zoom and dark mode

Each site's zoom is remembered, and dark mode turns off for a site that looks better as it is.

Permissions and notifications

Camera, microphone, location

Asked once, remembered, revocable. Location goes through two doors: the site asks you, and macOS asks Wuji — Wuji tells you which one is missing. Only the tab you're looking at can ask.

In Notification Center

A site asks once; the answer is remembered with the others. A click brings you back to the tab that sent it, and the site is written under the title, so none can pass itself off as another. Only from an encrypted page: rewritten on public Wi-Fi, a clear-text page can't notify in the site's name.

Even those WebKit was losing

WebKit doesn't hand the app the notifications from Gmail, Slack or Discord. Wuji relays them — the permission stays WebKit's, and the frame's origin is checked. Nothing in a private window.

Questions, in one place

Camera, location, installing a script, saving a password: a bubble at the bottom right. Closing without answering means “no”. Only one at a time, never swapped under the pointer, and a yes given in the first half second doesn't count.

The padlock and certificates

Two lines, five doors

The essentials read without clicking: who the connection is encrypted with, and who vouches for it. The rest waits behind its group — Identity, Validity, Encryption, SHA-256 Fingerprint, Chain of Trust —, and each line can be copied in one click.

Certificates no one vouches for

Common for a service you host yourself. The error page shows the certificate and its SHA-256 fingerprint, then offers to continue — for this host and this session only, never written to disk.

The address of the page you see

A page that starts a navigation by itself only shows its address on arrival: a script can't put “bank.com” above its own content. A click, going back or a typed address show up right away.

Uploading a file

A “choose a file” field opens the system panel as a sheet on the window, with its favorites and its search.
